# Ocean Change (2026)

Status date: November 15, 2026.

## Coral bleaching

| Global event | Years | Reef area at bleaching-level heat stress |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1998 | 21% |
| 2nd | 2010 | 37% |
| 3rd | 2014–2017 | 68.2% |
| **4th** | **2023–2025** | **84.4%**, mass bleaching in ≥83 countries/territories |

- NOAA confirmed the 4th event on 15 Apr 2024. Severe bleaching in Western Australia in early 2025 likely closed it. NOAA confirmed the end only after no widespread bleaching occurred in the Dec 2025–Feb 2026 austral summer (update of 2 June 2026).
- **Era of near-annual bleaching:** discrete "global events" are becoming hard to define. Every strong El Niño since 1998 has coincided with a global event, and a building El Niño raised concern again in mid-2026.
- **Link to the deep:** mesophotic and deep-water corals are not refuges by default. Their exposure depends on whether heat reaches them through mixing.

## AMOC (Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation)

- **Observed:** weakening at four latitudes over two decades (University of Miami study, April 2026).
- **Projected (Portmann et al. 2026, *Sci. Adv.*):**
  - Combining models with temperature and salinity observations gives **43–59% weakening by 2100** (central ≈51%). That is about 60% stronger than the multi-model average.
  - Aggressive, sustained emission cuts → about 20% weakening.
  - Greenland meltwater is not included, so this may be an underestimate.
- **Committed collapse?** A July 2026 ensemble study (Holden et al.) argues there is a significant probability that collapse is already committed. The IPCC AR6 view remains that collapse before 2100 is "very unlikely" (medium confidence).
- **Why the deep cares:** the AMOC ventilates the deep North Atlantic with oxygen and carbon. Weakening reduces deep oxygen supply and changes where heat and carbon are stored.

## Physical baseline (see `png/pressure_light_profile_generated.png`)

- Pressure ≈ 1 atm + 1 atm per ~10 m, so ~1,090 atm at Challenger Deep.
- Light decays roughly exponentially. In clear ocean, about 1% remains near 100 m, and photosynthesis effectively stops around 200 m.

## Open Questions

- How quickly do surface heatwaves propagate into the twilight zone (200–1,000 m)?
- Will AMOC weakening measurably deoxygenate deep-sea habitats such as cold-water coral reefs this century?
