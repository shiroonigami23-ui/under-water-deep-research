# Sweetman et al. (2024): Evidence of dark oxygen production at the abyssal seafloor

- **Citation:** Sweetman A.K., et al. (incl. F. Geiger, J.J. Marlow). *Nature Geoscience* (published 22 July 2024). doi:10.1038/s41561-024-01480-8
- **Access:** 🔓 Open access
- **Analysis basis:** abstract, Springer Nature/SAMS releases, coverage of both critiques and responses. Full text not read here.

## Question
Why did benthic chambers on nodule fields repeatedly record oxygen *increasing* when deep-sea sediments normally only consume O₂?

## Method
- Benthic lander chamber incubations at ~4,000 m in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone. The chambers enclose seafloor and seawater and log O₂ for up to several days.
- Cross-checked with a second O₂ measurement method, ex-situ incubations of nodules, and voltage measurements on nodule surfaces.
- The research was partly funded by mining companies, including TMC.

## Key Results
- In nearly all nodule-field incubations, O₂ rose steadily over ~2 days. Regions without nodules did not show this.
- Up to **0.95 V** was measured on single nodule surfaces. Clustered nodules could plausibly sum higher, past the ~1.23–1.5 V needed to split seawater.
- **Hypothesis:** a "geobattery". Electrochemical seawater splitting on metal-rich nodules releases O₂ (and H₂).

## Strengths
- The anomaly persisted across years and recalibrations, and appeared with two measurement methods.
- It gives a testable mechanism with clear predictions (H₂ co-production, voltage dependence).

## Limitations and Critiques
- **Methodological challenge (TMC and others):** possible artefacts such as trapped air bubbles or sensor/chamber effects.
- **Energetics:** how could nodules millions of years old sustain enough current? "Batteries run out."
- Scale is unknown. The authors themselves say the regional significance can't be judged from this study.
- **Response:** the **Dark Oxygen Research Initiative (DORI)**, launched Jan 2026 with US$5.2M from the Nippon Foundation, will deploy new landers in another Pacific area. They will measure O₂ and H₂ production, conductivity, turbidity and seafloor respiration, and test microbial versus electrochemical drivers.

## My Take
Treat this as a **strong anomaly with an unproven mechanism**. The key discriminator is hydrogen. Electrolysis must make H₂ alongside O₂ in roughly a 2:1 ratio, while a sensor artefact would not. DORI's 2026 lander data is the result to watch, and the tracker lists it.

## Follow-ups
- Simultaneous H₂/O₂ measurements; voltage over time; controls with nodules removed.
- Microbial versus abiotic partitioning (sterilised incubations).
