At mid-ocean ridges, back-arc basins and hotspots, seawater seeps into cracks in young crust, is heated by magma to **350–400 °C**, picks up metals and sulfide, and gushes back out.

## Vent types

| Type | Temperature | Look | Chemistry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Black smoker** | 350–400 °C | Black clouds of metal sulfide particles; tall chimneys | Acidic, metal- and sulfide-rich |
| **White smoker** | 100–300 °C | Lighter particles (barium, calcium, silicon) | Cooler, often at field margins |
| **Diffuse flow** | 5–100 °C | Shimmering water from cracks | Supports most of the biomass |
| **Alkaline/serpentinite** (e.g. Lost City) | 40–90 °C | White carbonate towers up to 60 m | Hydrogen- and methane-rich from rock–water reactions |

Fluids stay liquid at these temperatures because of the immense pressure.

## Cold seeps

Seeps occur along continental margins and trenches where methane- and sulfide-rich fluids leak slowly from sediments. They are cooler and longer-lived than vents, with features such as **methane hydrate mounds**, **brine pools** and authigenic carbonate pavements.

## Mineral deposits

Vent chimneys build **seafloor massive sulfide (SMS)** deposits rich in copper, zinc, gold and silver, which is why vents attract mining interest (see [Deep-sea mining](/wiki/impacts/deep-sea-mining.html)).
