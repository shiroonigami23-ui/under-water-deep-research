title: Ocean Heat: The Planet's Thermometer
summary: The ocean absorbs over 90% of the extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases. 2025 set a ninth straight record, with about 23 ZJ added in one year.
level: Intermediate
---
The ocean is where global warming really accumulates. **Over 90%** of the excess energy trapped by greenhouse gases ends up in seawater. That makes **ocean heat content (OHC)** one of the most reliable indicators of climate change, because it is far less noisy than air temperature[^og].

:::data 2025 in numbers (Pan, Cheng et al. 2026, Adv. Atmos. Sci.)
- Upper 2,000 m OHC rose by **~23 ± 8 zettajoules** relative to 2024[^ucar].
- That made 2025 the **ninth consecutive record year**, the longest streak on record[^eos].
- The 2024 gain was about 16 ZJ (other estimates put it near 13 ZJ), so 2025 was markedly larger[^eos][^mb].
- 23 ZJ is roughly **200 times** humanity's annual electricity consumption[^og].
- Regions at record heat included the tropical and South Atlantic, Mediterranean, North Indian Ocean and Southern Ocean[^eos].
- This happened despite developing **La Niña** conditions, which usually cool the surface[^ucar].
:::

## Why heat goes deep

Argo floats measure the upper 2,000 m, and most added heat sits there. But heat is now detected in the abyss too, carried down where deep water forms near Antarctica and in the North Atlantic. Deep Argo floats (to 6,000 m) are extending the record.

## Consequences

- **Sea-level rise:** warm water expands. Thermal expansion is a major share of sea-level rise.
- **Marine heatwaves:** more frequent, longer and deeper, driving [coral bleaching](/wiki/climate/coral-bleaching.html) and mass die-offs.
- **Stronger storms:** warmer surface water fuels tropical cyclones.
- **Less oxygen:** warmer water holds less gas and stratifies more (see [Deoxygenation](/wiki/climate/deoxygenation.html)).
- **Habitat shifts:** species move poleward and deeper.

## Open question

How quickly do surface heatwaves propagate into the twilight zone (200–1,000 m), where most fish biomass lives? Recent compound-change analyses show deep-reaching changes over 60 years, but direct observation remains sparse[^mb].
