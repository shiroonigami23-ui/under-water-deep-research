---
title: Ocean Acidification
summary: The ocean has absorbed about a quarter of human CO₂ emissions, and in 2025 scientists concluded the acidification planetary boundary was crossed around 2020.
level: Intermediate
---
When CO₂ dissolves in seawater it forms carbonic acid. This lowers pH and, critically, reduces the **carbonate ions** that corals, shellfish, pteropods and many plankton need to build shells and skeletons. The usual measure is **aragonite saturation state (Ω<sub>arag</sub>)**.

:::warning Planetary boundary crossed
Findlay et al. (2025, *Global Change Biology*) reassessed the ocean acidification boundary, defined as a 20% drop in Ω<sub>arag</sub> from pre-industrial levels. They found the global ocean **entered the boundary's uncertainty range by 2020**[^oaicc].

- **Over 40%** of the surface ocean and **up to 60%** of the upper 200 m had crossed it[^oaicc].
- Suitable habitat has fallen by **43%** for tropical and subtropical coral reefs, **up to 61%** for polar pteropods and **13%** for coastal bivalves[^oaicc].
- The authors propose a safer **10%** boundary, which the entire surface ocean passed **around 2000**[^oaicc].
:::

The 2025 Planetary Health Check also counted ocean acidification as crossed, bringing the total to seven of nine boundaries[^de].

## Why the deep is exposed

Subsurface waters are changing **faster than expected**, so impacts may be worse than surface data suggest. The **aragonite saturation horizon**, below which shells dissolve, is getting shallower. That squeezes the habitat of **cold-water corals** such as *Desmophyllum pertusum*, many of which already live close to this horizon. Polar waters are most affected because cold water dissolves more CO₂.

## Who is affected

| Organism | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pteropods (sea butterflies) | Shell dissolution; key food for salmon and whales |
| Corals (shallow and deep) | Slower calcification; weaker reef frameworks |
| Oysters, mussels, scallops | Larval failure; documented hatchery losses |
| Coccolithophores, foraminifera | Thinner plates; changes to the carbon pump |
| Fish | Altered senses and behaviour in some experiments (debated) |

## Outlook

Without rapid emission cuts, nearly all of the surface ocean is projected to exceed safe acidity levels by 2100 under mid- to high-emission scenarios[^tv]. Unlike warming, acidification cannot be offset by reflecting sunlight. Only cutting CO₂ fixes it.
