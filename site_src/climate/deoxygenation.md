Oxygen loss is the least-known of the ocean's three climate stressors (with warming and acidification), but it may be just as dangerous.

:::data Key measurements
- Global ocean oxygen fell by **more than 2%** (4.8 ± 2.1 petamoles) between 1960 and 2010, from a total of ~227 petamoles (Schmidtko et al. 2017, *Nature*)[^nat].
- The volume of **oxygen-free water** in the open ocean has **quadrupled**[^nd].
- Oxygen minimum zones at 200 m expanded by **~4.5 million km²**, roughly 60% of Australia's area[^clex].
- Coastal areas at risk of dead zones have grown **more than tenfold** since 1950[^nd].
- Losses could reach **up to 7%** by 2100[^vn].
:::

## Why oxygen is falling

1. **Solubility:** warm water holds less gas. This explains about **15%** of the loss[^nat].
2. **Stratification:** a warmer surface layer mixes less with deeper water, so less oxygen is carried down. Weaker overturning (see [AMOC weakening](/wiki/climate/amoc-weakening.html)) adds to this.
3. **Nutrient pollution (coastal):** fertiliser and sewage fuel algal blooms whose decay consumes oxygen.

## Oxygen minimum zones (OMZs)

OMZs are natural mid-water layers (roughly 100–1,000 m) beneath productive surface waters, especially in the eastern tropical Pacific, Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal. As they expand:

- **Tuna, billfish and sharks** are squeezed into shallower water, where they are easier to catch.
- Microbes in OMZs produce **nitrous oxide**, a potent greenhouse gas, and remove fixed nitrogen.
- Specialists such as the **vampire squid** thrive in low oxygen, but most animals cannot.

## The deep connection

Deep water gets oxygen only where it sinks from the surface at high latitudes. Slower overturning means deep habitats such as **cold-water coral reefs** could lose oxygen, though the size of that effect this century is still debated.

## Can it reverse?

Some models suggest OMZs may start shrinking from 2150–2300, but only after centuries[^ps]. Reducing nutrient runoff can shrink coastal dead zones quickly. Open-ocean deoxygenation needs emission cuts.
