The **Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework** (2022) set **Target 3**: conserve at least 30% of land and sea by 2030 ("30×30").

:::data Where we stand (2026)
- **10.01%** of the ocean is officially designated as protected or conserved. That threshold was first passed in April 2026, up from 8.6% in 2024[^iucn].
- About **5 million km²** was added in two years, an area larger than the EU[^iucn].
- Only about **3.3%** is **fully or highly protected**, with extraction prohibited (MPAtlas, March 2026)[^mb].
- National waters are 23.2% covered, but only **1.7%** of areas beyond national jurisdiction are[^aa].
- Only **1.3%** of the ocean has protected areas whose management effectiveness has been assessed[^iucn].
:::

## Paper parks

Many MPAs allow fishing, including bottom trawling. In the EU, a large share of MPAs are commercially trawled. Strongly protected reserves, by contrast, hold on average several times more fish biomass than fished areas nearby, and "spillover" can benefit neighbouring fisheries.

## What makes MPAs work

1. **Protection level:** fully or highly protected areas deliver most of the benefits.
2. **Size and age:** large, old, well-enforced reserves perform best.
3. **Enforcement:** satellite vessel tracking (such as Global Fishing Watch) helps.
4. **Connectivity:** networks matter because larvae and migrants cross boundaries.
5. **Climate-smart design:** protecting refuges and corridors as species shift.

## Deep-sea MPAs

Large remote reserves such as Papahānaumokuākea (USA), the Ross Sea MPA (Antarctica) and the Pitcairn Islands protect huge areas of deep habitat. The **High Seas Treaty** finally offers a route to protect the open ocean and international seabed (see [Who governs the deep?](/wiki/impacts/high-seas-treaty-and-isa.html)).
