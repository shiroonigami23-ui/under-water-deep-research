---
title: Bottom Trawling
summary: Dragging weighted nets across the seabed is the most widespread physical disturbance of the deep, with large costs for habitats and possibly the climate.
level: Intermediate
---
**Bottom trawls** and **dredges** are heavy nets and frames dragged across the seafloor to catch fish and shellfish that live on or near the bottom. In terms of area, bottom trawling is the **largest human physical disturbance of the seabed**.

## Impacts on habitats

- **Habitat destruction:** cold-water corals, sponge grounds and seamount communities can be flattened in a single pass. Recovery takes decades to millennia.
- **Bycatch:** large amounts of non-target animals are caught and discarded.
- **Sediment resuspension:** plumes smother filter feeders and change seabed chemistry.

## Carbon

:::debate The trawling carbon question
Sala et al. (2021, *Nature*) estimated that trawling disturbs sediment carbon equivalent to about **1 Gt of CO₂ per year**, comparable to global aviation[^yahoo]. They also found that protecting about **4% of the ocean**, mostly within national waters, would remove 90% of that risk[^ucsb]. Critics argue that much of the remineralised carbon stays in the water rather than reaching the atmosphere. Follow-up work (Atwood et al. 2024) estimated that a large share does reach the atmosphere within about a decade. The magnitude is still debated.
:::

A 2026 European study (*Ocean & Coastal Management*) estimated that trawling in EU, UK, Norwegian and Icelandic waters emits **~112 Mt CO₂ per year** and costs society up to **€16 billion annually**, about 90 times the fleet's profits[^og]. About **23%** of trawling effort takes place inside European MPAs[^sfs].

## Recovery when trawling stops

After a trawling ban in the UK's **Lyme Bay**, reef species increased by 95% and juvenile lobsters by 400%[^og]. The European Commission aims to phase out bottom-contact fishing in protected areas by 2030[^sfs].

## Deep-sea specifics

- Deep-sea fish such as **orange roughy** and **roundnose grenadier** grow slowly and mature late, so stocks collapse quickly.
- The UN General Assembly (resolutions from 2006 onwards) calls for protecting **vulnerable marine ecosystems (VMEs)**. The EU banned bottom trawling below 800 m in its waters in 2016.
