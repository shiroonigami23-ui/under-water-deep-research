## The legal zones (UNCLOS, 1982)

| Zone | Distance from coast | Who controls what |
|---|---|---|
| Territorial sea | 0–12 nm | Full coastal-state sovereignty (with innocent passage for ships) |
| Contiguous zone | 12–24 nm | Customs, immigration and pollution enforcement |
| **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)** | Up to 200 nm | Coastal state controls fishing, oil, gas and minerals |
| Continental shelf | Can extend beyond 200 nm | Seabed resources belong to the coastal state |
| **High seas** | Beyond EEZs | Water column open to all; covers over 60% of the ocean surface |
| **"The Area"** | Seabed beyond national jurisdiction | Minerals are the "common heritage of mankind", managed by the ISA |

## The International Seabed Authority (ISA)

The ISA is based in Kingston, Jamaica, with 169 members plus the EU. It has issued about 30 **exploration** contracts for nodules, crusts and sulfides, including **India's** contracts in the Central Indian Ocean Basin and the Indian Ocean ridge. It has never approved commercial exploitation. Its **Mining Code** was still not adopted after the 31st session ended in July 2026. See [Deep-sea mining](/wiki/impacts/deep-sea-mining.html).

## The High Seas Treaty (BBNJ Agreement)

:::data Status (November 2026)
- Adopted 19 June 2023. Reached its 60th ratification on 19 September 2025.
- **Entered into force on 17 January 2026**, with 86 parties by March 2026[^ec][^eli].
- The first Conference of the Parties (**COP1**) is due by about January 2027.
:::

It has four pillars:

1. **Area-based management tools**, including high-seas **marine protected areas**. Until now there was no general mechanism to create them.
2. **Environmental impact assessments** for activities in international waters.
3. **Marine genetic resources:** sharing benefits from commercial uses of high-seas organisms (for example in drugs and enzymes).
4. **Capacity building and technology transfer** for developing countries.

## Open questions

- How BBNJ protected areas will interact with the ISA, fisheries bodies and the IMO, each of which keeps its own mandate.
- Whether the US national mining route undermines UNCLOS (the US is not a party).
- Whether 30×30 can be reached on the high seas, where only about 1–2% is currently protected (see [Marine protected areas](/wiki/impacts/marine-protected-areas.html)).
