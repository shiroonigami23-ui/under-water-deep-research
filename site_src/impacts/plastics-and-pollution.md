## The deep is a plastic sink

Floating plastic accounts for only a small fraction of what enters the ocean. Much of the "missing" plastic ends up on the **seafloor**[^rsos].

:::facts Key findings
- A plastic bag was found at **10,898 m** in the Mariana Trench, the deepest recorded debris[^gwr].
- CSIRO estimated about **14 million tonnes** of microplastics on the seafloor globally[^csiro].
- In 2025, **turbidity currents** were directly observed carrying microplastics from continental shelves down submarine canyons to the deep sea[^nioz].
- Microplastics were found in almost all sampled vent **mussels and snails** at ~2,000 m in the Indian and southwest Pacific oceans (2026)[^mb].
- A 2025 *Nature* synthesis mapped microplastics through the whole water column, a "smog" from the surface to the seabed[^earth].
:::

## How plastic gets down

1. **Biofouling:** microbes and algae colonise particles, which then sink.
2. **Marine snow and faecal pellets** package microplastics and carry them down quickly.
3. **Turbidity currents and canyons** funnel debris downslope. Canyon biodiversity hotspots are now also microplastic hotspots[^nioz].
4. **Bottom currents** sort and concentrate fibres in sediment drifts.

## Other pollutants

- **PCBs and PBDEs:** found in hadal amphipods at concentrations comparable to some polluted coasts.
- **Mercury:** reaches deep food webs via sinking particles.
- **"Forever chemicals" (PFAS):** carried on microplastic surfaces[^nioz].
- **Munitions and chemical weapons** dumped after the World Wars in the Baltic and elsewhere.
- **Noise:** shipping, seismic surveys and (potentially) mining.

## Why it matters

Deep-sea animals live long and grow slowly, so pollutants accumulate. Deep plastics are effectively impossible to retrieve, which makes prevention at source the only real solution.
