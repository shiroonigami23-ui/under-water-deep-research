## Four eras

1. **Sounding and dredging (1870s–1920s):** ropes, wires and nets. The Challenger expedition dispelled the "azoic hypothesis" that no life exists below ~550 m.
2. **Acoustics and bathyscaphes (1920s–1960s):** echo sounders revealed the shape of ocean basins, including the mid-ocean ridges that led to plate tectonics. *Trieste* proved humans could reach the bottom.
3. **Submersibles and discovery (1964–2000s):** *Alvin* and its peers found vents, seeps and whale-fall communities. ROVs took over much routine work.
4. **Full-depth and data era (2010s–now):** repeatable full-ocean-depth crewed dives, autonomous vehicles, telepresence expeditions streamed live, eDNA and AI-assisted video annotation.

## Lessons

Every era overturned an assumption: that the deep was lifeless, that life needs sunlight, that trenches are barren, that fish can live anywhere. Yet only about **0.001%** of the deep seafloor has been seen[^bell]. See [How little we have seen](/wiki/exploration/how-little-we-have-seen.html).

[^bell]: Bell et al. 2025, *Science Advances* — <https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adp8602>
