# Timeline of Deep-Ocean Science

<div class="timeline">
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1521</span>: Magellan attempts to sound the Pacific with a ~700 m line and fails to find the bottom.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1818</span>: John Ross brings up worms and a basket star from ~1,800 m in Baffin Bay, early evidence of deep life.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1843</span>: Edward Forbes proposes the "azoic hypothesis": no life below ~550 m.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1858</span>: The first transatlantic telegraph cable is laid. Cable repairs later bring up deep animals.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1872–76</span>: The HMS Challenger expedition founds oceanography and describes ~4,700 new species.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1930–34</span>: Beebe and Barton's Bathysphere reaches 923 m.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1938</span>: A living coelacanth is discovered off South Africa.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1951</span>: Challenger II sounds the Challenger Deep.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1960</span>: Trieste reaches the bottom of the Mariana Trench.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1964</span>: DSV Alvin is commissioned.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1977</span>: Hydrothermal vent communities are discovered (Galápagos Rift).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1979</span>: The first black smokers are seen (East Pacific Rise), and a nodule test-mining track is cut in the CCZ (revisited in 2023).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1982</span>: UNCLOS is signed. It enters into force in 1994, the year the ISA is founded.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1984</span>: Cold seeps are discovered in the Gulf of Mexico.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1987</span>: The first natural whale-fall community is found (Santa Catalina Basin).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">1998</span>: The first global coral bleaching event (21% of reefs).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2000</span>: Lost City hydrothermal field is discovered; the Argo float programme begins.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2004</span>: The RAPID array begins monitoring the AMOC.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2010</span>: The Census of Marine Life concludes; 2nd global bleaching event.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2012</span>: Cameron's solo Challenger Deep dive; the giant squid is filmed alive at depth.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2014–17</span>: 3rd global bleaching event (68.2% of reefs).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2017</span>: Seabed 2030 launches (6% mapped).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2019</span>: Five Deeps Expedition reaches the deepest point of every ocean.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2020</span>: Fendouzhe reaches the Challenger Deep.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2022</span>: Deepest fish filmed (8,336 m); the Kunming-Montreal 30×30 target is agreed.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2023</span>: The High Seas Treaty is adopted (19 June).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2023–25</span>: 4th global bleaching event (84.4% of reefs).</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2024</span>: "Dark oxygen" paper published; Fendouzhe hadal surveys.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2025</span>: Peng et al.: life at 9,533 m. Bell et al.: ≤0.001% seen. Ocean acidification boundary declared crossed. Ocean heat sets its 9th straight record.</div>
<div class="ev"><span class="yr">2026</span>: High Seas Treaty in force (17 Jan); 10% of the ocean protected (Apr); Seabed 2030 at 28.7%; Ocean Census reports 1,121 new species; ISA session ends without a Mining Code; NOAA hearing on the first US commercial mining application.</div>
</div>
