Hadal Life: Living at the Bottom of the World

Trenches deeper than 6,000 m host snailfish, giant amphipods and, as of 2025, flourishing chemosynthetic communities at 9,533 m.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 2 min read🔗 3 sources🎓 Advanced

The hadal zone, the ocean trenches from 6,000 to ~11,000 m, makes up a tiny fraction of the seafloor but spans almost half the ocean’s depth range. Each trench is an isolated, extreme habitat. Many host endemic species.

Key records
  • Deepest fish filmed: a Pseudoliparis snailfish at 8,336 m, Izu-Ogasawara Trench (2022)1.
  • Deepest fish caught: P. belyaevi at 8,022 m, Japan Trench1.
  • Deepest chemosynthetic animal community: tubeworms and bivalves at 9,533 m, Kuril–Kamchatka Trench (Nature, 2025)2.
  • Deepest plastic bag: 10,898 m, Mariana Trench3.

Who lives there#

  • Snailfishes: the top fish predators, translucent and scaleless.
  • Amphipods: scavenging crustaceans. Hirondellea gigas in the Challenger Deep can digest wood using enzymes. Giant amphipods (Alicella gigantea) reach ~30 cm.
  • Holothurians (sea cucumbers): often dominate trench floors by biomass.
  • Xenophyophores: giant single-celled organisms.
  • Chemosynthetic communities: siboglinid tubeworms and bivalves fed by methane and sulfide seeping along faults in the bending plate2.

The 2025 discovery#

The crewed submersible Fendouzhe surveyed the Kuril–Kamchatka and western Aleutian trenches in July–August 2024. It found chemosynthesis-based communities over ~2,500 km at 5,800–9,533 m, with high sediment methane likely made by microbes in place2. That changes the picture of trench ecosystems from food-starved basins to places that can host methane-powered oases. Full analysis: Peng et al. 2025.

Why trenches funnel food (and pollution)#

The V-shape of trenches concentrates organic matter sliding down the slopes, and earthquakes trigger sediment avalanches. That supports surprising biomass, but it also concentrates pollutants. PCBs and microplastics have been found in hadal amphipods.

Exploration#

Only a handful of crewed dives have reached the deepest points: Trieste (1960), Deepsea Challenger (2012), Limiting Factor (2019 onwards) and Fendouzhe (2020 onwards). See History of exploration.
