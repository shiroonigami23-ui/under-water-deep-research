Deep Ocean Circulation

How cold, salty water sinks at the poles and slowly ventilates the entire deep ocean, and why the AMOC is the conveyor's most watched link.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 2 min read🔗 3 sources🎓 Intermediate

The deep ocean is not stagnant. Water that sinks in a few polar regions spreads along the seafloor across entire basins, rises slowly, and eventually returns to the surface. One full loop takes on the order of a thousand years. This thermohaline circulation (driven by temperature, “thermo”, and salinity, “haline”) carries oxygen into the deep, stores heat and carbon, and shapes climate.

How water sinks#

Seawater gets denser when it gets colder or saltier. In winter, two regions make water dense enough to sink to the abyss:

  • North Atlantic (Nordic, Labrador and Irminger seas): strong cooling makes North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW), which flows south at ~1,500–4,000 m.
  • Around Antarctica: sea-ice formation rejects salt into very cold water, forming Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW), the densest water in the ocean. It hugs the seafloor and spreads north into every basin.

That sinking water carries dissolved oxygen taken up at the surface. It is why most of the deep ocean is oxygenated at all. Where ventilation is weak, oxygen minimum zones form (see Deoxygenation).

The AMOC#

The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation is the Atlantic part of this system. Warm surface water (including the Gulf Stream) flows north, releases heat to the atmosphere (keeping north-western Europe mild), cools, sinks, and returns south at depth.

Measured and projected
  • Observations from arrays at four latitudes show the AMOC weakening over the past two decades (University of Miami study, April 2026)1.
  • An observation-constrained projection finds 43–59% weakening by 2100. That is about 60% stronger than the average climate model2. Full analysis: Portmann et al. 2026.

Why the deep ocean cares#

  1. Oxygen supply. Less sinking means less oxygen delivered to the deep North Atlantic.
  2. Heat and carbon storage. The overturning pushes heat and anthropogenic CO₂ into the interior. Changes alter where and how much is stored.
  3. Larval connectivity. Deep currents carry the larvae of vent, seep and coral species between isolated habitats.

Upwelling: the return path#

Deep water returns to the surface mainly in the Southern Ocean, where westerly winds draw it up along sloping density surfaces, and through slow mixing elsewhere. Coastal upwelling (Peru, California, Benguela) brings nutrient-rich water up and fuels some of the world’s richest fisheries. Beneath these productive zones lie some of the largest oxygen minimum zones.

Open questions#

  • How close is the AMOC to a tipping point? Some studies argue collapse may already be committed3. The IPCC’s AR6 assessment still calls collapse before 2100 “very unlikely” (medium confidence).
  • How will Antarctic Bottom Water formation respond to Antarctic meltwater? Several studies report it slowing.

See the full climate treatment in AMOC weakening.
