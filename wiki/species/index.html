🐠 Species Atlas

Profiles of ocean animals, plants and microbes from the sunlit reef to the hadal trenches: habitat, depth, feeding and hunting, mating, migration and behaviour.

72 entries in 16 groups.

Cephalopods

Chambered nautilus

A shelled cephalopod lineage almost unchanged for ~500 million years, with buoyancy control by gas chambers.

Cephalopods

Colossal squid

The heaviest invertebrate known, with swivelling hooks on its arms; first filmed alive (a juvenile) in 2025.

Cephalopods

Dumbo octopuses

The deepest-living octopuses, flapping ear-like fins; one was filmed at nearly 7,000 m.

Cephalopods

Giant Pacific octopus

The largest octopus species, known for problem-solving and devoted, fatal motherhood.

Cephalopods

Giant squid

The source of kraken legends. First filmed alive in its deep habitat in 2012 and again in 2019 using a glowing lure.

Cephalopods

Vampire squid

Neither squid nor octopus: a gentle detritus eater living in the oxygen minimum zone.

Cnidarians

Atolla jellyfish

A deep-red crown jelly famous for its 'burglar alarm' display of spinning blue light.

Cnidarians

Cold-water coral (Lophelia)

Reef-building coral that thrives in dark, cold water without symbiotic algae.

Cnidarians

Giant siphonophore

A colonial organism that rivals the blue whale in length, made of specialised zooids.

Cnidarians

Reef-building corals

Animals that partner with algae to build the largest biological structures on Earth.

Crustaceans

Antarctic krill

Possibly the most abundant wild animal by biomass (hundreds of millions of tonnes), and the base of the Antarctic food web.

Crustaceans

Giant isopod

A deep-sea relative of the woodlouse that can survive years between meals.

Crustaceans

Japanese spider crab

The largest arthropod by leg span.

Crustaceans

Yeti crab

A hairy vent crab that farms chemosynthetic bacteria on its arms.

Deep-sea fish

Barreleye (spookfish)

A fish with a transparent, fluid-filled head and tubular eyes that can rotate to look up through it.

Deep-sea fish

Blobfish

Famous as the 'world's ugliest animal', but only because it decompresses when hauled to the surface.

Deep-sea fish

Humpback anglerfish

The classic deep-sea anglerfish: a lure of glowing bacteria, a cavernous mouth and dwarf males.

Deep-sea fish

Lanternfishes

Possibly the most abundant vertebrates on Earth; their nightly migration is the largest on the planet.

Deep-sea fish

Mariana snailfish

A pale, scaleless top predator of the Mariana Trench, among the deepest-living fishes known.

Deep-sea fish

Orange roughy

A slow-growing deep-sea fish that can live past 150 years; its fishery became a cautionary tale.

Deep-sea fish

Pacific blackdragon

Ultra-black skin, a glowing chin barbel, and males that never feed as adults.

Deep-sea fish

Pacific viperfish

A fanged midnight-zone predator whose teeth are too long to fit inside its own mouth.

Deep-sea fish

Pelican eel (gulper eel)

An eel that is mostly mouth: a loose pouch-like jaw much larger than its body.

Deep-sea fish

Tripod fish

Stands on three elongated fin rays facing into the current, waiting for food to drift into its mouth.

Deep-sea fish

West Indian Ocean coelacanth

A 'living fossil' lobe-finned fish thought extinct for 66 million years until found off South Africa in 1938.

Echinoderms

Crown-of-thorns starfish

A venomous coral-eating starfish whose population outbreaks devastate reefs.

Echinoderms

Deep-sea brittle stars

Among the most abundant animals on the deep seafloor, carpeting entire slopes.

Echinoderms

Sea pig

A deep-sea sea cucumber that walks on tube feet, often in herds.

Marine mammals

Blue whale

The largest animal ever known to have lived.

Marine mammals

Cuvier's beaked whale

The record-holding mammal diver: 2,992 m deep and a dive lasting 222 minutes.

Marine mammals

Dugong

A strictly marine herbivore and possible inspiration for mermaid legends.

Marine mammals

Humpback whale

Famous for complex songs, bubble-net feeding and some of the longest mammal migrations.

Marine mammals

Narwhal

The 'unicorn whale', whose tusk is a sensory tooth with millions of nerve endings.

Marine mammals

Northern elephant seal

Spends ~90% of its time at sea underwater, making dive after dive to over 1,000 m.

Marine mammals

Sea otter

A keystone species that protects kelp forests by eating sea urchins.

Marine mammals

Sperm whale

The deepest-diving large predator, with the biggest brain on Earth; it hunts giant squid in the dark.

Microbes

Bioluminescent dinoflagellates

The cause of glowing 'sea sparkle' waves on beaches at night.

Microbes

Deep subseafloor microbes

A vast biosphere buried kilometres below the seabed, living on geological timescales.

Microbes

Prochlorococcus

The most abundant photosynthetic organism on Earth, discovered only in 1988.

Molluscs

Scaly-foot snail

The only animal known to build its armour from iron sulfide; the first species listed as endangered by deep-sea mining.

Open-ocean fish

Atlantic bluefin tuna

A warm-bodied ocean racer that crosses the Atlantic and dives into the twilight zone.

Open-ocean fish

Atlantic cod

The fish behind one of history's most famous fishery collapses (Grand Banks, 1992).

Open-ocean fish

Atlantic salmon

Returns from the ocean to its home river to spawn, guided by smell.

Open-ocean fish

European eel

Crosses the Atlantic to spawn in the Sargasso Sea, a journey never directly observed.

Open-ocean fish

Ocean sunfish

The world's heaviest bony fish and the most fecund vertebrate known.

Open-ocean fish

Swordfish

Heats its eyes and brain to hunt in cold, dark water.

Plants & algae

Diatoms

Glass-walled microalgae that produce a large share of the oxygen we breathe.

Plants & algae

Giant kelp

A brown alga that grows up to ~60 cm a day and forms underwater forests.

Plants & algae

Seagrasses

Flowering plants that returned to the sea and store carbon far faster than land forests per hectare.

Reef fish

Bluestreak cleaner wrasse

Runs 'cleaning stations' and passes the mirror test, a sign of self-recognition.

Reef fish

Clown anemonefish

Lives among stinging anemone tentacles and can change sex from male to female.

Reef fish

Giant moray

Has a second set of 'alien' jaws in its throat and hunts cooperatively with groupers.

Reef fish

Mandarinfish

One of the few vertebrates with true blue pigment cells.

Reef fish

Parrotfishes

Reef gardeners that grind coral rock into the white sand of tropical beaches.

Reef fish

Red lionfish

A venomous Indo-Pacific fish that became one of the worst marine invasions in the Atlantic.

Reef fish

Seahorses

The only animals where the male gets pregnant.

Sea turtles & reptiles

Green sea turtle

A herbivorous turtle that 'mows' seagrass meadows and navigates using Earth's magnetic field.

Sea turtles & reptiles

Leatherback sea turtle

The largest and deepest-diving turtle, which keeps warm in cold water and eats almost only jellyfish.

Sea turtles & reptiles

Marine iguana

The only lizard that forages in the sea.

Seabirds

Emperor penguin

The deepest-diving bird, breeding through the Antarctic winter on sea ice.

Seabirds

Wandering albatross

The largest wingspan of any bird; circles the globe using dynamic soaring.

Sharks & rays

Frilled shark

An eel-like shark with 300 needle teeth, often called a living fossil.

Sharks & rays

Giant oceanic manta ray

The largest ray, with the largest brain-to-body ratio of any fish, and dives deep into the twilight zone.

Sharks & rays

Goblin shark

Its jaws catapult forward at high speed to snatch prey, one of the fastest jaw projections among sharks.

Sharks & rays

Great white shark

Apex predator famous for ocean-crossing migrations and dives into the twilight zone.

Sharks & rays

Greenland shark

The longest-lived vertebrate known: radiocarbon dating of eye lenses suggests ages of several centuries.

Sharks & rays

Whale shark

The largest fish alive, a gentle filter feeder that dives to nearly 2 km.

Sponges & others

Venus' flower basket

A glass sponge whose silica lattice inspires engineers, and a lifelong home for a pair of shrimp.

Worms & vent fauna

Bone-eating worms

Mouthless worms that bore into whale bones using acid and live off bacteria.

Worms & vent fauna

Deep-sea meiofauna (nematodes & kin)

The most numerous animals in deep-sea sediments, found even at the bottom of the Challenger Deep.

Worms & vent fauna

Giant tube worm

The icon of hydrothermal vents: a mouthless, gutless worm fed entirely by internal bacteria.

Worms & vent fauna

Pompeii worm

One of the most heat-tolerant complex animals, living in tubes on black-smoker chimneys.
