1 · ISA Mining Code
The International Seabed Authority's 31st session ran in two Council parts (9–19 March and 13–24 July 2026) plus the Assembly (27–31 July). On 24 July the Council agreed a **roadmap** of remaining work but **did not adopt** the exploitation regulations, so no commercial mining has been approved in international waters. **Watch:** the 32nd session (2027) and whether a deadline for the Code is set. **Read more:** [Deep-sea mining](/wiki/impacts/deep-sea-mining.html) · [Who governs the deep?](/wiki/impacts/high-seas-treaty-and-isa.html) **Sources:** [ENB summary](https://enb.iisd.org/international-seabed-authority-isa-council-31-2-summary) · [Benchmark Minerals](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/benchmark-at-the-international-seabed-authority-meeting-as-global-commercial-framework-remained-unresolved)
2 · US deep-sea mining (NOAA / TMC)
NOAA's rule combining an exploration licence and commercial recovery permit took effect on 21 January 2026. TMC USA filed for about **65,000 km²** of the Clarion-Clipperton Zone. NOAA found the application **fully compliant** and held a public hearing on **13 October 2026**. TMC says it expects a permit by the end of Q1 2027. **Watch:** NOAA's certification decision, then the Environmental Impact Statement and its comment period. **Sources:** [NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-advances-review-of-first-ever-deep-seabed-mining-commercial-recovery-application) · [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1644737/noaa-determines-tmc-usas-consolidated-deep-seabed-mining-application-is-in-full-compliance)
3 · High Seas Treaty (BBNJ)
The treaty entered into force on **17 January 2026** and had 86 parties by March. The first Conference of the Parties must meet within a year, and Belgium, Chile and China are bidding to host the secretariat. **Read more:** [Who governs the deep?](/wiki/impacts/high-seas-treaty-and-isa.html) · [Marine protected areas](/wiki/impacts/marine-protected-areas.html) **Sources:** [European Commission](https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/news/high-seas-treaty-enters-force-milestone-ocean-conservation-2026-01-16_en) · [ELI](https://www.eli.org/vibrant-environment-blog/charting-new-waters-high-seas-treaty-after-two-months)
4 · Dark oxygen
The 2024 claim that nodules produce oxygen through seawater electrolysis remains contested. The **Dark Oxygen Research Initiative (DORI)**, a US$5.2M programme, is deploying landers that measure hydrogen and oxygen together. **Read more:** [Sweetman et al. 2024 analysis](/wiki/library/sweetman-2024.html) **Sources:** [SAMS DORI](https://sams.ac.uk/science/projects/dori/) · [Nippon Foundation](https://tnfsa.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/13238305)
5 · Hadal life
*Fendouzhe* dives found chemosynthetic communities over about 2,500 km of the Kuril–Kamchatka and Aleutian trenches, down to **9,533 m**. **Read more:** [Hadal life](/wiki/life/hadal-life.html) · [Peng et al. 2025 analysis](/wiki/library/peng-2025.html) **Source:** doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09317-z · [Nature Asia highlight](https://www.natureasia.com/ja-jp/nature/pr-highlights/15298)
6 · Species discovery (Ocean Census)
Year 3 (Apr 2025 – Mar 2026) recorded **1,121** species new to science, 54% above the usual rate. About 728 came through Species Discovery Awards, many from museum backlogs. Formal description usually takes ~13.5 years, which is why the NOVA platform records "discovered" as a status. **Read more:** [Species discovery](/wiki/life/species-discovery.html) **Sources:** [Nekton](https://nektonmission.org/news/scientists-discover-over-1100-new-marine-species-in-landmark-ocean-census/) · [Scientific American](https://scientificamerican.com/article/ocean-census-reveals-more-than-1-100-new-species)
7 · Seafloor mapping (Seabed 2030)
**28.7%** of the seafloor was mapped by April 2026, with almost five million km² added in a year. **Read more:** [Mapping the seafloor](/wiki/exploration/mapping-the-seafloor.html) **Source:** [IHO](https://iho.int/en/global-seabed-mapping-reaches-new-milestone-as-five-million-square-kilometres-added-in-a-year)
8 · Visual exploration
At most **0.001%** of the deep seafloor has ever been imaged, and 65% of observations lie near the USA, Japan and New Zealand. **Read more:** [How little we have seen](/wiki/exploration/how-little-we-have-seen.html) · [Bell et al. 2025 analysis](/wiki/library/bell-2025.html)
9 · Coral bleaching
The fourth global bleaching event reached **84.4%** of reefs (2023–2025). NOAA confirmed its end on 2 June 2026. Researchers describe an era of near-annual bleaching. **Read more:** [Coral bleaching](/wiki/climate/coral-bleaching.html) **Sources:** [NOAA Coral Reef Watch](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/research/coral_bleaching_report.php) · [GCRMN](https://gcrmn.net/2026/06/08/4gbe-ended/)
10 · AMOC
Weakening has been observed at four latitudes. Portmann et al. project **43–59% weakening by 2100**, and a July 2026 study argues collapse may already be committed. **Read more:** [AMOC weakening](/wiki/climate/amoc-weakening.html) · [Portmann et al. 2026 analysis](/wiki/library/portmann-2026.html) **Sources:** [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/climate-change/nations-need-to-prepare-now-key-atlantic-ocean-current-is-much-closer-to-collapse-than-scientists-thought) · [Arctic Today](https://www.arctictoday.com/amoc-collapse-may-already-be-locked-in-new-study-suggests/)
Next data-pull targets
- NOAA certification / EIS notice for TMC USA. - BBNJ COP1 date, venue and secretariat decision. - DORI first results; any comments or replies on dark oxygen. - GCRMN *Status of Coral Reefs of the World: 2025* report. - Seabed 2030 June 2027 figure.
