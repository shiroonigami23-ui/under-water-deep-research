🌊 Tyrrhenian Sea

Mediterranean Sea

A deep, volcanic part of the Mediterranean west of Italy.

TypeSea
Part ofMediterranean Sea

Max depth (m)3785

🧭 At a glance

Tyrrhenian Sea covers about 275,000 km², averages 2,000 m deep and reaches 3,785 m at Tyrrhenian Basin. This encyclopedia links 45 species to it, including Angelshark, Atlantic torpedo, Common octopus, Turbot, Anoxic loriciferan, Atlantic bonito, Barrel jellyfish, Brill.

Overview#

A deep part of the western Mediterranean bounded by Italy, Sicily, Sardinia and Corsica.

Geography#

Includes the Bay of Naples and the Aeolian Islands.

Seafloor and geology#

Reaches about 3,785 m with large underwater volcanoes such as Marsili, Europe’s biggest.

Water and currents#

Gyres and deep water.

Climate, weather and cyclones#

Mediterranean.

Flora and fauna#

Pelagos Sanctuary for whales in the north.

Ecosystems#

The Pelagos Sanctuary for whales (shared with the Ligurian Sea).

History and exploration#

Etruscan and Roman sea routes; Vesuvius buried Pompeii (79 CE).

People and economy#

Ports of Naples and Palermo and tourism.

Threats and protection#

Plastic hotspots (microplastics up to ~1.9 million particles per m² of seafloor).
