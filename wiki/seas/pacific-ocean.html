🌊 Pacific Ocean

World Ocean

The largest and deepest ocean, covering about a third of Earth's surface.

🧭 At a glance

Pacific Ocean covers about 165,250,000 km², averages 4,000 m deep and reaches 10,935 m at Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench. This encyclopedia links 614 species to it, including Abyssal grenadier, Abyssobrotula galatheae, Albacore, Banded pipefish, Barreleye (spookfish), Basking shark, Bigfin squid, Black swallower. Wrecks and discoveries here: Truk Lagoon fleet.

Overview#

The Pacific is the largest single feature on Earth’s surface: at about 165 million km² it is bigger than all the land combined and holds roughly half of the planet’s free water. It stretches about 15,500 km from the Bering Strait to Antarctica and nearly 20,000 km from Indonesia to Colombia. Its name, ‘peaceful sea’, came from Magellan in 1520, but it is the most geologically violent ocean, ringed by the Ring of Fire. Its tropical belt generates the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, the most important source of year-to-year climate variability on the planet.

Geography#

Bounded by Asia and Australia to the west, the Americas to the east and the Southern Ocean to the south (about 60°S). Marginal seas include the Bering, Okhotsk, Japan, Yellow, East and South China, Philippine, Coral and Tasman seas. More than 25,000 islands lie in the Pacific, more than in all other oceans combined, grouped into Melanesia, Micronesia and Polynesia.

Seafloor and geology#

The Pacific plate is the largest tectonic plate and is being consumed at subduction zones on almost every side. That creates the deepest trenches on Earth: Mariana (Challenger Deep, ~10,935 m), Tonga (~10,800 m), Philippine, Kuril–Kamchatka, Kermadec and Japan trenches. The East Pacific Rise spreads up to ~15 cm a year, the fastest on Earth, and hosts black-smoker vents. Tens of thousands of seamounts and guyots dot the basin, including the Hawaiian–Emperor chain that records the plate’s motion over a hotspot. The Clarion-Clipperton Zone between Hawaii and Mexico holds the world’s richest polymetallic nodule fields.

Water and currents#

Two great gyres (North and South Pacific) circulate clockwise and anticlockwise. The Kuroshio is the Pacific’s Gulf Stream; the California and Humboldt currents carry cold water toward the equator and drive rich upwelling. The tropical Pacific warm pool is the warmest large body of ocean water. Every 2–7 years El Niño weakens the trade winds and warms the eastern Pacific, while La Niña does the opposite; the swings shift rainfall, cyclones and fisheries worldwide. The eastern tropical Pacific holds the largest oxygen minimum zone in the ocean.

Climate, weather and cyclones#

The western North Pacific produces more tropical cyclones (typhoons) than any other basin, about a third of the global total, including the strongest storms on record. El Niño years bring floods to Peru, drought to Australia and Indonesia, and weaker Indian monsoons. The Pacific Decadal Oscillation shifts sea temperatures over decades, and the 2014–16 ‘Blob’ marine heatwave killed seabirds, whales and kelp along North America.

Flora and fauna#

The Coral Triangle (Indonesia, Philippines, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands) holds about 76% of all known coral species and the world’s richest reef fish diversity. The Great Barrier Reef is the largest living structure on Earth. Kelp forests line California and Chile. Upwelling off Peru supports the anchoveta, the largest single-species fishery. The open Pacific holds tuna, blue sharks, leatherback turtles, albatrosses and the largest whales; its trenches hold snailfish and the deepest known chemosynthetic communities.

Ecosystems#

Coral reefs and atolls, kelp forests, mangroves of Southeast Asia, upwelling systems (California, Humboldt), hydrothermal vents (East Pacific Rise, back-arc basins), hadal trenches, seamounts, abyssal nodule plains, and the oligotrophic central gyres that are the ocean’s ‘deserts’.

History and exploration#

Polynesian navigators settled the Pacific’s islands over thousands of years using stars, swells and birds, reaching Hawaii, Rapa Nui and New Zealand. Magellan crossed in 1520–21; Cook mapped much of it in the 1770s. HMS Challenger sounded the Mariana area in 1875, Trieste reached Challenger Deep in 1960, and Alvin found the first hydrothermal vents on the Galápagos Rift in 1977. The Pacific was the main theatre of naval warfare in World War II, leaving thousands of wrecks, from Pearl Harbor to Truk Lagoon.

People and economy#

About half of the world’s fish catch comes from the Pacific, including tuna, pollock, anchoveta and squid. It carries the busiest container trade routes between Asia and the Americas. Pacific island nations have vast Exclusive Economic Zones that make them ‘large ocean states’.

Threats and protection#

Coral bleaching (the Great Barrier Reef has had repeated mass bleaching), overfishing of bluefin tuna and sharks, the Great Pacific Garbage Patch, nuclear-test legacies (Bikini, Mururoa), deep-sea mining interest in the CCZ, and sea-level rise threatening atoll nations such as Tuvalu and Kiribati.
