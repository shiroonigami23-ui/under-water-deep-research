🌊 Labrador Sea

Atlantic Ocean

A key site where the North Atlantic overturns, forming deep water.

TypeSea
Part ofAtlantic Ocean
Area (km²)841,000
Max depth (m)4316
Deepest pointCentral basin
Salinity~34.9 PSU

🧭 At a glance

Labrador Sea covers about 841,000 km² and reaches 4,316 m at Central basin. This encyclopedia links 92 species to it, including American lobster, Atlantic cod, Atlantic horseshoe crab, Atlantic menhaden, Bigclaw snapping shrimp, Caribbean spiny lobster, French angelfish, Giant hermit crab.

Overview#

An arm of the North Atlantic between Labrador and Greenland where deep water forms in winter.

Geography#

Bounded by Canada’s Labrador coast and southwest Greenland.

Seafloor and geology#

Reaches about 4,300 m.

Water and currents#

Winter cooling makes surface water sink, feeding the deep Atlantic.

Climate, weather and cyclones#

Icebergs from Greenland drift south (‘Iceberg Alley’).

Flora and fauna#

Harp seals, cod and seabirds.

Ecosystems#

Sea-ice and iceberg ecosystems.

History and exploration#

Vikings sailed past; the Titanic iceberg likely came via here.

People and economy#

Fisheries.

Threats and protection#

Freshening from Greenland melt may weaken deep-water formation.
