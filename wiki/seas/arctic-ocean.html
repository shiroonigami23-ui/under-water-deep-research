🌊 Arctic Ocean

🧭 At a glance

Arctic Ocean covers about 14,060,000 km², averages 1,205 m deep and reaches 5,550 m at Molloy Deep, Fram Strait. This encyclopedia links 193 species to it, including Abyssal grenadier, Abyssobrotula galatheae, Albacore, Atlantic cod, Basking shark, Bigfin squid, Black swallower, Blue shark. Wrecks and discoveries here: HMS Erebus, HMS Terror.

Overview#

The Arctic is the smallest (~15.6 million km²) and shallowest ocean, mostly enclosed by Eurasia and North America. Much of it is covered by sea ice that grows and shrinks with the seasons. It is warming about four times faster than the global average, so its ice cover is one of the clearest signals of climate change.

Geography#

Ringed by Russia, Norway, Greenland, Canada and Alaska. Marginal seas include the Barents, Kara, Laptev, East Siberian, Chukchi and Beaufort seas, plus Baffin Bay and Hudson Bay. The Fram Strait is the main deep connection to the Atlantic; the narrow Bering Strait links it to the Pacific.

Seafloor and geology#

The Arctic has the widest continental shelves on Earth, especially off Siberia, holding about half its area. The Lomonosov Ridge divides it into the Amerasian and Eurasian basins; the Gakkel Ridge is the slowest-spreading ridge on Earth yet hosts active vents (the Aurora field). The deepest point is the Molloy Deep (~5,550 m) in the Fram Strait.

Water and currents#

The Beaufort Gyre stores fresh water from rivers and melting ice, and the Transpolar Drift carries ice from Siberia toward Fram Strait. Huge Siberian rivers (Ob, Yenisei, Lena) make the surface fresher than any other ocean. Atlantic water entering through the Barents Sea is warming the Arctic from below (‘Atlantification’).

Climate, weather and cyclones#

September sea-ice extent has fallen by about 13% per decade since satellite records began in 1979, and multi-year ice has largely disappeared. Loss of white ice exposes dark water that absorbs more sunlight, accelerating warming (ice–albedo feedback). Thawing subsea permafrost may release methane.

Flora and fauna#

Polar bears, walruses, ringed and bearded seals, narwhals, belugas and bowhead whales, polar cod living in ice cracks, ice algae, Arctic char, little auks and ivory gulls. The Bering and Barents seas host some of the richest fisheries on Earth.

Ecosystems#

Sea-ice ecosystems, polynyas (open-water oases in ice), shallow Siberian shelves, the Barents Sea cod system and vents on the Gakkel Ridge.

History and exploration#

Inuit and other Indigenous peoples have lived along its coasts for millennia. Searches for the Northwest Passage killed Franklin’s 1845 expedition; its ships Erebus and Terror were found in 2014 and 2016. Nansen’s Fram drifted across it in 1893–96; the submarine USS Nautilus passed under the North Pole in 1958.

People and economy#

Indigenous subsistence hunting, Barents Sea and Bering Sea fisheries, Arctic shipping routes opening as ice melts (the Northern Sea Route), and oil and gas exploration.

Threats and protection#

Rapid ice loss, Atlantification, shipping and oil-spill risk, microplastics trapped in sea ice, and fisheries moving north into unregulated waters (a central Arctic fishing moratorium was agreed in 2018).
