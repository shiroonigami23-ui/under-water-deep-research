🌊 South China Sea

Pacific Ocean

A biodiverse and contested sea crossed by a large share of global shipping.

🧭 At a glance

South China Sea covers about 3,500,000 km², averages 1,212 m deep and reaches 5,016 m. This encyclopedia links 424 species to it, including Banded pipefish, Barreleye (spookfish), Blob sculpin, Blobfish, Blue tang, Bluestreak cleaner wrasse, Chambered nautilus, Chinook salmon.

Overview#

A marginal sea of about 3.5 million km² in the western Pacific, rich in reefs, fisheries and possible oil and gas. It carries roughly a third of global maritime trade and is the scene of overlapping territorial claims.

Geography#

Bordered by China, Taiwan, the Philippines, Malaysia, Brunei, Indonesia and Vietnam, and containing the Spratly and Paracel islands and Scarborough Shoal.

Seafloor and geology#

Its central basin reaches about 5,016 m; it opened by seafloor spreading between ~32 and 16 million years ago.

Water and currents#

Monsoon winds reverse surface currents seasonally; the Kuroshio intrudes through the Luzon Strait.

Climate, weather and cyclones#

Typhoon-prone; driven by the East Asian monsoon.

Flora and fauna#

About a third of the world’s marine biodiversity by some estimates, giant clams, dugongs, green turtles and many reef fish.

Ecosystems#

Coral reefs at the edge of the Coral Triangle, seagrass, and some of the world’s most heavily fished waters.

History and exploration#

Maritime Silk Road trade; today about a third of global shipping passes through, and overlapping territorial claims make it a geopolitical flashpoint.

People and economy#

Fisheries employing millions and shipping through the Strait of Malacca.

Threats and protection#

Island-building that destroyed reefs, giant-clam poaching, overfishing and maritime confrontations.
