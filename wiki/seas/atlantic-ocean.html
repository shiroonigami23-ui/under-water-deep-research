🌊 Atlantic Ocean

World Ocean

The second-largest ocean, split down the middle by the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and home to the AMOC.

🧭 At a glance

Atlantic Ocean covers about 106,460,000 km², averages 3,646 m deep and reaches 8,376 m at Milwaukee Deep, Puerto Rico Trench. This encyclopedia links 438 species to it, including Abyssal grenadier, Abyssobrotula galatheae, Albacore, American lobster, Angelshark, Atlantic blue marlin, Atlantic bluefin tuna, Atlantic cod. Wrecks and discoveries here: RMS Titanic, Bismarck, Lusitania, Andrea Doria, Whydah Gally, SS Central America.

Overview#

The Atlantic is the second-largest ocean (about 106 million km²) and the youngest of the major basins, opening as Pangaea broke apart around 180 million years ago. It is still widening by about 2.5 cm a year along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. It is the saltiest open ocean and the engine room of the global overturning circulation: cold, salty water sinks in the north and spreads through the deep ocean worldwide. That makes the Atlantic central to Earth’s climate.

Geography#

An S-shaped basin between the Americas to the west and Europe and Africa to the east, from the Arctic to the Southern Ocean. Marginal seas include the Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico, Mediterranean, North, Baltic, Norwegian and Labrador seas, plus the Sargasso Sea, the only sea with no coastline. It drains more river water than any other ocean, including the Amazon, Congo, Mississippi and Niger.

Seafloor and geology#

The Mid-Atlantic Ridge runs ~16,000 km down the centre and rises above sea level in Iceland and the Azores. Vent fields such as TAG, Rainbow and the alkaline Lost City lie along it. The deepest point is the Milwaukee Deep in the Puerto Rico Trench (~8,376 m); the South Sandwich Trench lies in the far south. Wide continental shelves (Grand Banks, North Sea, Patagonian shelf) host rich fisheries, and submarine canyons cut the margins.

Water and currents#

The Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Current carry tropical heat to Europe. The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) sinks water in the Labrador and Nordic seas, forming North Atlantic Deep Water. In the south, the Benguela upwelling off Namibia is one of the most productive systems on Earth. Salinity reaches ~37 PSU in the subtropics because evaporation exceeds rainfall.

Climate, weather and cyclones#

The tropical Atlantic produces hurricanes from June to November, born from African easterly waves over warm water above ~26.5°C. The North Atlantic Oscillation steers European winters. A weakening AMOC is one of the major climate tipping-point concerns (see AMOC weakening).

Flora and fauna#

Cold-water coral reefs off Norway and Ireland, the cod and herring stocks of the Grand Banks and North Sea, North Atlantic right whales, humpbacks breeding in the Caribbean, Atlantic bluefin tuna crossing the basin, the Sargasso Sea’s floating sargassum community where European and American eels spawn, sea turtles, gannets, puffins, and the Lost City vent fauna.

Ecosystems#

Sargassum mats, coral reefs (Caribbean), cold-water coral mounds, seagrass and mangroves, upwelling systems (Benguela, Canary), vent and seep communities, and the abyssal plains of the Cape Verde and Sohm basins.

History and exploration#

Vikings reached North America around 1000 CE; Columbus crossed in 1492. The Atlantic slave trade forcibly carried over 12 million Africans across it. Matthew Maury charted its winds and currents in the 1850s; the first transatlantic telegraph cable was laid in 1858. Famous wrecks include the Titanic (found in 1985 at ~3,800 m), Lusitania and Bismarck. Endurance, lost in the Weddell Sea in 1915, was found in 2022.

People and economy#

Historic cod, herring, tuna and whale fisheries; North Sea oil and offshore wind; the busiest transatlantic shipping and cable routes.

Threats and protection#

Collapse of the Grand Banks cod (1992), AMOC weakening, sargassum blooms swamping Caribbean beaches since 2011, ship strikes and entanglement of right whales, plastic accumulation in the North Atlantic gyre, and warming that pushes fish north.
