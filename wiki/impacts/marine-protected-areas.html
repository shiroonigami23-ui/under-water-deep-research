Marine Protected Areas and 30×30

10% of the ocean is now officially protected, but only about 3% is fully or highly protected, and the high seas lag far behind.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 2 min read🔗 3 sources🎓 Introductory

The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (2022) set Target 3: conserve at least 30% of land and sea by 2030 (“30×30”).

Where we stand (2026)
  • 10.01% of the ocean is officially designated as protected or conserved. That threshold was first passed in April 2026, up from 8.6% in 20241.
  • About 5 million km² was added in two years, an area larger than the EU1.
  • Only about 3.3% is fully or highly protected, with extraction prohibited (MPAtlas, March 2026)2.
  • National waters are 23.2% covered, but only 1.7% of areas beyond national jurisdiction are3.
  • Only 1.3% of the ocean has protected areas whose management effectiveness has been assessed1.

Paper parks#

Many MPAs allow fishing, including bottom trawling. In the EU, a large share of MPAs are commercially trawled. Strongly protected reserves, by contrast, hold on average several times more fish biomass than fished areas nearby, and “spillover” can benefit neighbouring fisheries.

What makes MPAs work#

  1. Protection level: fully or highly protected areas deliver most of the benefits.
  2. Size and age: large, old, well-enforced reserves perform best.
  3. Enforcement: satellite vessel tracking (such as Global Fishing Watch) helps.
  4. Connectivity: networks matter because larvae and migrants cross boundaries.
  5. Climate-smart design: protecting refuges and corridors as species shift.

Deep-sea MPAs#

Large remote reserves such as Papahānaumokuākea (USA), the Ross Sea MPA (Antarctica) and the Pitcairn Islands protect huge areas of deep habitat. The High Seas Treaty finally offers a route to protect the open ocean and international seabed (see Who governs the deep?).
