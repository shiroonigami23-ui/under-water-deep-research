Plastics and Pollution in the Deep

From a plastic bag at 10,898 m to microplastics inside vent snails, the deep sea is the final sink for much of the pollution we create.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 2 min read🔗 6 sources🎓 Intermediate

The deep is a plastic sink#

Floating plastic accounts for only a small fraction of what enters the ocean. Much of the “missing” plastic ends up on the seafloor1.

Key findings
  • A plastic bag was found at 10,898 m in the Mariana Trench, the deepest recorded debris2.
  • CSIRO estimated about 14 million tonnes of microplastics on the seafloor globally3.
  • In 2025, turbidity currents were directly observed carrying microplastics from continental shelves down submarine canyons to the deep sea4.
  • Microplastics were found in almost all sampled vent mussels and snails at ~2,000 m in the Indian and southwest Pacific oceans (2026)5.
  • A 2025 Nature synthesis mapped microplastics through the whole water column, a “smog” from the surface to the seabed6.

How plastic gets down#

  1. Biofouling: microbes and algae colonise particles, which then sink.
  2. Marine snow and faecal pellets package microplastics and carry them down quickly.
  3. Turbidity currents and canyons funnel debris downslope. Canyon biodiversity hotspots are now also microplastic hotspots4.
  4. Bottom currents sort and concentrate fibres in sediment drifts.

Other pollutants#

  • PCBs and PBDEs: found in hadal amphipods at concentrations comparable to some polluted coasts.
  • Mercury: reaches deep food webs via sinking particles.
  • “Forever chemicals” (PFAS): carried on microplastic surfaces4.
  • Munitions and chemical weapons dumped after the World Wars in the Baltic and elsewhere.
  • Noise: shipping, seismic surveys and (potentially) mining.

Why it matters#

Deep-sea animals live long and grow slowly, so pollutants accumulate. Deep plastics are effectively impossible to retrieve, which makes prevention at source the only real solution.
